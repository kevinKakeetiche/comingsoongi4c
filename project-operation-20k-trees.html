Project profile · Ongoing

Restoring Community Forests, Biodiversity & Environmental Resilience

Community-led reforestation in Machatoum, Massangam, Cameroon.

2023–2028Implementation period
20,000Tree target
2,300Trees planted to date
4Tree species

Location: Machatoum community, Massangam, West Region, Cameroon. Status: ongoing. Progress figures are reported by GI4C.

Community context

The community challenge

Community consultations and a needs assessment conducted by GI4C highlighted concerns about the progressive loss of tree cover and its effects on the local environment.

Residents reported increased exposure to heat and sunlight, loss of vegetation and shade, soil degradation and erosion, and reduced habitat for wildlife. They also expressed concern about the loss of trees valued for food, cultural significance, ancestral heritage and their role as community landmarks.

These concerns identified reforestation as a community priority: not simply to increase the number of trees, but to contribute to environmental restoration, biodiversity conservation and the long-term well-being of the community.

GI4C’s response

From community concerns to action

GI4C initiated Operation 20K Trees in response to environmental challenges identified through community engagement. The initiative aims to contribute to the restoration of tree cover in Machatoum through the planting and establishment of 20,000 trees.

GI4C leads the initiative, including planning, financing, community engagement, tree planting and documentation, with engagement involving local community leadership and relevant administrative and forestry authorities.

Reported progress

Implementation and progress

2,300 trees planted

GI4C reports that 2,300 trees have been planted across four species as part of the ongoing initiative.

Four species established

Reported planting includes Ayous (1,200), Sapelli (500), Bubinga (300) and Iroko (300).

Longer-term objectives

The initiative seeks to restore vegetation, increase tree cover and support the longer-term conservation of biodiversity and natural resources. Ecological outcomes require continued monitoring and evidence over time.

Next steps and collaboration

Sustaining implementation

Reaching the 20,000-tree target requires sustained implementation, appropriate care of planted trees and continued attention to their establishment and survival. The next phase focuses on continuing planting activities, supporting tree establishment and documenting progress toward the initiative’s objectives.

GI4C welcomes collaboration with environmental organizations, forestry experts, local authorities, development partners and potential funders interested in community reforestation, biodiversity conservation and environmental sustainability. Partners can contribute technical expertise, resources for planting and tree maintenance, environmental monitoring and community engagement.

Our vision: A community where environmental restoration contributes to healthier surroundings, protected natural heritage and a more sustainable future for generations to come.
